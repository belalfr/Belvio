How Google finds, crawls and indexes your pages

14 Oct 20266 min readBy Belal, Founder

When someone searches on Google, they are not searching the live web. They are searching Google's copy of it. A page can exist, load perfectly in your browser and still be missing from that copy. Understanding how pages get in is the first step of any SEO work.

Step 1: discovery

Google has to find a URL before it can do anything with it. It finds pages in three main ways: by following links from pages it already knows, by reading the sitemap you submit, and by being told directly, for example through Search Console. A page that nothing links to and that isn't in a sitemap is invisible, however good it is.

Step 2: crawling

Crawling means Google's bot, Googlebot, requests the page the way a browser does and downloads what comes back. Two things control this on your side. The first is robots.txt, a small file at the root of your site that tells crawlers which paths they may request. The second is how your server answers: a 200 means the page is fine, a 404 means it's gone, a 301 means it moved, and a 5xx means your server failed. A slow or unreliable server makes Google crawl less.

A page blocked in robots.txt can't be read, so Google can't see what's on it. That is different from keeping it out of search.

Step 3: rendering

Many modern pages build their content with JavaScript. Google downloads the page, runs the scripts and looks at the result. If important text only appears after a script that fails, or after a click, Google may never see it. The simplest check is to compare what you see in the browser with what Google's tools report back for the same URL.

Step 4: indexing

After crawling and rendering, Google decides whether to store the page in its index. Plenty of crawled pages are not stored. Common reasons are a noindex tag on the page, a canonical tag pointing to a different URL, content that duplicates another page, or content Google judges to add little. Only indexed pages can appear in search results.

Step 5: ranking

When someone searches, Google picks from its index the pages that best match the search and orders them. Everything above is the entry ticket. Nothing in the first four steps guarantees a good position, but a failure in any of them guarantees you won't have one.

How to check your own pages

  1. Open Google Search Console and paste a page address into the inspection bar at the top.
  2. Read the verdict. It tells you whether the page is in Google's index and, if not, the reason it gives.
  3. Use the live test to see the page as Googlebot sees it after rendering.
  4. Check your robots.txt by opening yourdomain.com/robots.txt and confirming that nothing important is blocked.
  5. Check that your sitemap lists the pages you want found and nothing you don't.

Most "my page won't rank" problems turn out to be one of these five steps failing quietly. That is why the first thing I do on any project is a crawl: it shows which step each page is stuck on.
